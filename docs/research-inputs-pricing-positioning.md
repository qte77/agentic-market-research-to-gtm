# Research inputs: agent pricing and positioning

Disposition of the opinion posts collected in
[issue #21](https://github.com/qte77/agentic-market-research-to-gtm/issues/21)
(2026-10-22). The posts are opinion without data and their authors sell
related products, so they serve as prompts for our own analysis, not as
evidence. Checked on 2026-10-29.

## 1. Agent pricing ("Stop Selling Agents Like Software", 2026-10-21)

Hypothesis from the post: agents are costly to run, so charge for work done
(metered first, then outcomes) rather than per feature. Its three market
examples had no sources; each was checked against first-party pricing pages.

- **Devin sells a pool of metered work.** [Devin pricing](https://devin.ai/pricing)
  shows flat plans (Free, Pro, Max, Teams, Enterprise) with a usage allowance
  that refreshes daily and weekly, and extra usage at API rates. No named work
  unit appears on the page. Verdict: partly holds, as a subscription plus
  metered overage rather than a pure pool of work. Fold in that way.
- **Clay sells a pool of metered work.** [Clay pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)
  shows plans with two metered units, Actions (platform usage) and Data
  Credits (vendor data and AI). Headline and FAQ prices on the page disagree.
  Verdict: holds. Fold in; do not quote plan prices.
- **Figma moves its new agent onto credits.**
  [Figma pricing](https://www.figma.com/pricing/) shows AI features sharing
  one credit pool with a per-seat monthly allotment, usage varying by request,
  and purchasable extra credits. The page does not say which plans include the
  agent. Verdict: holds for credits; the "moving" (change over time) part is
  not verifiable from the page, so drop the timing claim.

Not verified: the post's pricing ladder (metered, then outcome, then revenue
share) has no evidence behind it, and none of the three examples sits beyond
the metered rung. Treat it as an unproven hypothesis for the GTM pricing stage.

Decision: the pricing stage may use "seat or subscription base plus metered
credits" as a pattern, citing the three first-party pages above. The ladder
and the author's claims are dropped.
